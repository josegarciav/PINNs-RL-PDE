# Learned and adaptive controllers for PINN training (RL, bandits, meta-learning, learned optimizers)

Reading conventions used throughout: "PR" = peer-reviewed venue confirmed on a page I retrieved; "preprint" = arXiv only as far as I could confirm. Numbers are quoted as published, with the setup. "Equal compute" is stated only when the paper itself matched wall-clock time or residual evaluations; otherwise the basis of comparison is named. Research date: 2026-11-19.

## 1. Adaptive collocation sampling, and RL / bandit collocation selection: key papers and equal-compute evidence

### Takeaway
Residual-driven sampling (RAD, RAR-D, R3) has real, replicated evidence of beating fixed uniform points at an equal number of residual points and equal iterations, but almost none of it is at equal wall-clock or equal residual evaluations, and the strongest simple baseline, uniform resampling every few iterations, closes most of the gap on several problems and wins on some. RL-based collocation selection exists only as 2025-2026 preprints with per-problem agents, single runs and weak baselines; I found no bandit formulation of collocation selection at all.

### Cited Findings

**Hand-designed adaptive sampling (the baselines a learned controller must beat)**

- Wu, Zhu, Tan, Kartha, Lu, "A comprehensive study of non-adaptive and residual-based adaptive sampling for PINNs", CMAME vol. 403, 2023 (PR; arXiv July 2022). Ten sampling methods, four forward and two inverse problems, more than 6,000 PINN runs; introduces RAD (resample all points from a density that is a nonlinear function of the residual) and RAR-D. — [arXiv 2207.10289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10289v1)
- Wu et al., forward problems, L2 relative error (geometric mean ± SD, at least 10 runs), same number of residual points per column: Burgers (2000 points) Random 13.3 ± 8.35 %, Hammersley 3.02 ± 2.98 %, Random-R (uniform with resampling) 1.69 ± 1.67 %, RAR-G 0.12 ± 0.04 %, RAD 0.02 ± 0.00 %, RAR-D 0.03 ± 0.01 %. Allen-Cahn (1000 points) Random 22.2 ± 16.9 %, Hammersley 0.14 ± 0.14 %, Random-R 0.55 ± 0.34 %, RAD 0.08 ± 0.06 %. Wave (2000 points) Random 68.4 ± 20.1 %, Random-R 0.72 ± 0.90 %, RAR-G 0.81 ± 0.11 %, RAD 0.09 ± 0.04 %. Diffusion (30 points) Random 0.74 ± 0.17 %, Random-R 0.12 ± 0.06 %, RAD 0.11 ± 0.07 %. — [ar5iv 2207.10289](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10289)
- Wu et al., inverse problems: KdV (600 points) solution error Random 8.86 ± 2.80 %, Random-R 0.97 ± 0.15 %, RAD 0.77 ± 0.11 %, RAR-G 8.83 ± 1.98 %; inferred λ1 error Random-R 0.41 ± 0.30 % vs RAD 0.31 ± 0.19 %. Diffusion-reaction (15 points) k(x) error Random-R 3.43 ± 1.80 % vs RAD 2.76 ± 1.32 %; RAR-G is worse than plain Random here (15.9 ± 1.53 % vs 5.77 ± 2.05 %). — [ar5iv 2207.10289](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10289)
- Wu et al. compare at an equal number of residual points (and in places equal total iterations), not at equal wall-clock or equal residual evaluations. The only cost statement is qualitative: "the RAD method is more computationally costly than the RAR-D methods with the same number of residual points"; RAD needs residual evaluations on a dense candidate set and that cost is not quantified. The authors recommend RAD "when the number of residual points is small". — [ar5iv 2207.10289](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10289)
- Daw, Bu, Wang, Perdikaris, Karpatne, "Mitigating Propagation Failures in PINNs using Retain-Resample-Release (R3) Sampling", ICML 2023, PMLR 202:7264-7302 (PR). Claims accumulation of points in high-residual regions "with little to no computational overhead" because residuals are evaluated only on the current Nr points, with no dense candidate pool. — [PMLR v202 daw23a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/daw23a.html)
- R3, Table 1, relative L2 error (%), Nr = 1000, 5 seeds, same iteration count per column. Convection β=30 at 100k iterations: PINN fixed 107.5 ± 10.9, PINN dynamic (fresh uniform samples every iteration) 2.81 ± 1.45, RAR-G 10.5 ± 5.67, RAD 3.35 ± 2.02, RAR-D 67.1 ± 4.28, R3 1.51 ± 0.26. Convection β=50 at 300k: PINN dynamic 56.9 ± 9.08, RAD 64.1 ± 1.98, R3 1.98 ± 0.72, Causal R3 2.28 ± 0.76. Allen-Cahn at 200k: PINN fixed 69.4 ± 4.02, PINN dynamic 0.77 ± 0.06, RAD 0.78 ± 0.05, R3 0.83 ± 0.15, Causal R3 0.71 ± 0.007. — [ar5iv 2207.02338](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.02338)
- In the R3 table, uniform resampling every iteration ties or beats R3 and RAD on Allen-Cahn (0.77 vs 0.83 vs 0.78) and beats RAR-G, RAR-D, L-infinity and curriculum regularisation on convection β=30; RAD and RAR-D, which win in Wu et al., are near-failures on convection β=50 (64.1 and 75.3). The comparison is at equal iterations and nominal point count; RAR-type methods additionally evaluate a dense set of roughly 100k to 1M points, so per-iteration compute is not equal. — [ar5iv 2207.02338](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.02338)
- Nabian, Gladstone, Meidani, "Efficient training of PINNs via importance sampling", Computer-Aided Civil and Infrastructure Engineering, 2021 (PR; DOI 10.1111/mice.12685). Points drawn each iteration with probability proportional to the loss; a piecewise-constant approximation of the loss makes sampling cheaper; tested on elasticity, diffusion and plane stress. — [arXiv 2104.12325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12325)
- Tang, Wan, Yang, "DAS-PINNs: A deep adaptive sampling method for solving high-dimensional PDEs", Journal of Computational Physics 476, 111868, 2023 (PR). Residual treated as an unnormalised density and approximated with a generative model (KRnet) that generates new collocation points; gains reported over uniform sampling especially for low-regularity problems in higher dimension. — [arXiv 2112.14038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.14038)
- Gao, Yan, Zhou, "Failure-informed adaptive sampling for PINNs" (FI-PINNs): a residual-based "failure probability" as error indicator, new points added in the failure region, with error bounds. The arXiv page (v3, Jan 2023) lists no journal reference. — [arXiv 2210.00279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00279)
- Lau, Hemachandra, Ng, Low, "PINNACLE: PINN Adaptive ColLocation and Experimental points selection", ICLR 2024 (PR). Jointly selects collocation, boundary/initial and experimental points with a criterion from the combined NTK eigenspectrum; reported to beat existing point-selection methods on forward, inverse and transfer problems. Not an RL method. — [ICLR 2024 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/19012); [arXiv 2404.07662](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.07662)
- Subramanian et al., "Adaptive self-supervision algorithms for PINNs" (arXiv 2207.04084; full author list not verified): moves a fixed budget of collocation points toward high-loss regions; reports up to an order of magnitude smaller error "with similar computational complexity as the baseline". — [arXiv 2207.04084 (Utah mirror)](https://www.sci.utah.edu/publications/Sub2022a/2207.04084.pdf)
- Florido, Wang, Khan, Jimack, "Investigating Guiding Information for Adaptive Collocation Point Sampling in PINNs", ICCS 2024, LNCS 14834 (PR). Burgers and Allen-Cahn; one of the few studies that fixes the number of residual evaluations; concludes that several guiding metrics can improve results at that fixed budget but that "no single approach is likely to be 'optimal'". — [arXiv 2404.12282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12282)
- More recent hand-designed alternatives exist (Hessian/quadrature-based sampling, Caradot et al. 2025; QR-DEIM point selection, Celaya et al. 2025), all preprints as retrieved. — [arXiv 2504.00910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00910v1); [arXiv 2501.07700](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07700v1)

**RL-based collocation selection**

- Zhenao Song, "RL-PINNs: Reinforcement Learning-Driven Adaptive Sampling for Efficient Training of PINNs", arXiv 2504.12949v1, 17 April 2025 (preprint, single author, no venue listed). DQN (two hidden layers [128, 64], γ = 0.95, experience replay, target network); state = current spatial position; action = discrete moves ±Δx/±Δy with step 0.1 or 0.2; reward = function variation |u(x_{t+1}) − u(x_t)| if above a threshold, else 0 (a gradient-free proxy rather than the residual); one round of sampling instead of repeated retrain-and-resample. — [arXiv 2504.12949](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12949v1)
- RL-PINNs reported table (single run per cell, no seeds or variance reported; sampling s / training s / relative L2). Burgers: UNIFORM 8.61e-4 / 1322.65 / 0.2896; RAR 0.18 / 1326.59 / 0.1323; RAD 0.18 / 1328.56 / 0.1474; RL-PINNs 16.18 / 1304.39 / 0.0534. Wave: UNIFORM 0.0423, RAR 0.0339, RAD 0.0351, RL-PINNs 0.0053 (sampling 7.01 s vs 0.28-0.31 s). High-dimension: UNIFORM 0.1426, RAR 0.0956, RAD 0.1250, RL-PINNs 0.0394 (sampling 35.45 s vs 0.82 s; training 2759.82 s vs 2811-2835 s). Biharmonic: UNIFORM 0.3265, RAR 0.1611, RAD 0.2340, RL-PINNs 0.0851. Dual-peak: UNIFORM 0.8624, RAR 0.3659, RAD 1.0889, RL-PINNs 0.1878. — [arXiv 2504.12949](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12949v1)
- RL-PINNs setup caveats: iterations are roughly matched (25,000 for RL-PINNs vs 5 rounds x 5,000 for baselines) and total wall-clock ends up similar, but the paper does not claim an equal-compute protocol; point counts differ (single-peak: 667 retained points vs 1,000 for baselines); the agent is trained inside each run, and transfer across equations is listed only as future work. — [arXiv 2504.12949](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12949v1)
- Siteng Kang and Xinhua Zhang, "Adaptive Data Harvesting for Efficient Neural Network Learning with Universal Constraints", arXiv 2605.09707, 10 May 2026 (preprint). An RL policy adjusts the training samples iteratively in response to the model's learning performance; validated on Lyapunov neural networks and PINNs; the abstract claims better constraint satisfaction and "significantly higher efficiency" with no numbers on the abstract page. A search summary describes an outer agent that observes residuals of candidate collocation sets and chooses a sampling ratio; I did not verify this in the full text. — [arXiv 2605.09707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09707)
- An open-source package, pinnrl (PyPI, v0.1.0), implements a DQN agent that steers sampling toward high-residual regions. It is software, not a paper with a controlled comparison. — [PyPI pinnrl 0.1.0](https://pypi.org/project/pinnrl/0.1.0/)
- Two targeted searches for bandit formulations (multi-armed bandit, UCB, Thompson sampling) of PINN collocation selection or loss weighting returned no such paper; the results were all hand-designed adaptive samplers. — [search hit list example: arXiv 2504.00910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00910v1)

### Inferences
- The baseline bar for any learned sampler is not fixed uniform points. It is (a) uniform resampling every iteration or every few iterations, which costs nothing, and (b) RAD and R3. In Wu et al. Random-R already removes one to two orders of magnitude of error relative to fixed Random on Burgers, Allen-Cahn and wave; in Daw et al. it matches the best adaptive method on Allen-Cahn.
- Rankings of the hand-designed samplers are not stable across papers: RAD is "always the best" in Wu et al. but fails on convection β=50 in Daw et al. and is worse than uniform on RL-PINNs' dual-peak problem (1.0889 vs 0.8624). This points to sensitivity to hyperparameters (the exponent and constant of the density, resampling period) and to the PDE, so a claimed win over "RAD" means little unless RAD was tuned.
- The RL-PINNs evidence is weak by the standards the user needs: one run per cell, baselines with 13-40 % relative error on Burgers and single-peak problems (suggesting untuned baselines), unequal point counts, and a per-problem agent. Its headline advantage (one-shot sampling) is an engineering property, not evidence that a learned policy generalises.
- Nobody appears to have reported a learned sampler against Random-R at equal residual evaluations with multiple seeds. That is a cheap, decisive experiment.
- The cost of an adaptive sampler is dominated by residual evaluations on a candidate pool; R3's design (no candidate pool) shows that the hand-designed frontier has already moved to "zero-overhead" methods, which raises the bar for any controller with inference cost.

### Gaps
- Equal wall-clock comparisons: none of Wu et al., Daw et al., DAS-PINNs, FI-PINNs or Nabian et al. were confirmed to report error at equal wall-clock or equal total residual evaluations. Daw et al. have a cost analysis in an appendix that I did not read. Nabian et al. argue convergence-speed gains but I did not retrieve their timing numbers.
- FI-PINNs journal venue (I believe SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing, 2023) and RAR's origin (Lu, Meng, Mao, Karniadakis, DeepXDE, SIAM Review 2021) were not verified on a retrieved page.
- DAS-PINNs numbers and the cost of training the KRnet generative model were not retrieved.
- Full text of Kang and Zhang (2605.09707) not read: state/action/reward, baselines, and whether the policy transfers across PDEs are unknown.
- PINNACLE numbers and its compute overhead (NTK eigenspectrum) not retrieved.
- I cannot rule out bandit-based collocation papers in venues that the search engine did not surface; "no result found" is from two searches.

## 2. Adaptive loss weighting, and learned or RL-based weighting

### Takeaway
Adaptive weighting is a crowded field of hand-designed rules (gradient-statistics annealing, NTK eigenvalues, trainable per-point weights, causal weights, ReLoBRaLo, gradient surgery such as ConFIG), each of which wins on its own benchmarks; independent benchmarking (PINNacle) shows task-specific gains, 1.5-3x runtime overhead, and no universal winner. I found no RL-agent or bandit weighting method for PINNs; the only "learned" weighting is meta-learned losses (Psaros et al.) and multi-objective search.

### Cited Findings

- Wang, Teng, Perdikaris, "Understanding and mitigating gradient pathologies in PINNs" (arXiv January 2020): unbalanced back-propagated gradients between loss terms; learning-rate annealing (LRA) balances terms using gradient statistics, plus a modified architecture; abstract reports accuracy improved "by a factor of 50-100x" across several problems. The arXiv page lists no journal reference. — [arXiv 2001.04536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04536)
- Wang, Yu, Perdikaris, "When and why PINNs fail to train: A neural tangent kernel perspective": NTK-derived weights; in an NTK-based study the theory-derived weights were reported to agree closely with weights found by manual tuning. — [arXiv 2007.14527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.14527)
- McClenny and Braga-Neto, "Self-Adaptive PINNs using a Soft Attention Mechanism", Journal of Computational Physics 474, 111722, 2023 (PR): trainable weight per training point, updated by gradient ascent; abstract claims lower L2 error than other state-of-the-art PINN algorithms "while using a smaller number of training epochs" (epochs, not wall-clock). — [arXiv 2009.04544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04544)
- Wang, Sankaran, Perdikaris, "Respecting causality is all you need for training PINNs" (arXiv March 2022): temporal causal weights; success claimed on Lorenz, chaotic Kuramoto-Sivashinsky and turbulent Navier-Stokes where earlier formulations fail. arXiv page lists no journal reference. — [arXiv 2203.07404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07404)
- Bischof and Kraus, "Multi-Objective Loss Balancing for Physics-Informed Deep Learning" (ReLoBRaLo), published in CMAME in 2025 per the ETH record (PR; arXiv October 2021). Notable because it includes a hand-tuned baseline: "we manually chose the optimal scalings λi through grid search". — [arXiv 2110.09813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09813); [ETH research collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/5fa2396d-0d66-41ae-9257-61b099f435c7)
- ReLoBRaLo forward problems, median validation loss on u (3-4 runs): Burgers grid-searched fixed weights 1.2e-3, GradNorm 2.0e-3, LRA 1.6e-4, SoftAdapt 8.1e-4, ReLoBRaLo 1.4e-4. Kirchhoff plate: fixed 1.3e-8, LRA 2.7e-9, ReLoBRaLo 4.0e-10. Helmholtz: fixed 7.1e-2, GradNorm 5.6e-6, LRA 1.4e-5, ReLoBRaLo 2.6e-5 (so GradNorm and LRA beat ReLoBRaLo there). Inverse Burgers parameter error: fixed 1.9e-10, LRA 2.5e-11, ReLoBRaLo 2.2e-10 (the fixed-weight baseline beats ReLoBRaLo). — [ar5iv 2110.09813](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.09813)
- ReLoBRaLo overhead, extra seconds per 1,000 steps relative to no balancing: Burgers (base 3.7 s) GradNorm +10.6, LRA +3.5, SoftAdapt +0.4, ReLoBRaLo +0.6; Kirchhoff (base 17.3 s) GradNorm +128.6, LRA +139.7, SoftAdapt +20.2, ReLoBRaLo +22.5; Helmholtz (base 4.8 s) GradNorm +10.4, LRA +6.7, SoftAdapt +5.0, ReLoBRaLo +5.2. Accuracy is compared at equal steps, with overhead reported separately. — [ar5iv 2110.09813](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.09813)
- Liu, Chu, Thuerey, "ConFIG: Towards Conflict-free Training of PINNs", ICLR 2025 spotlight (PR). Update direction with positive dot product with every loss-specific gradient. Baselines Adam, PCGrad, IMTL-G, LRA, MinMax, ReLoBRaLo; PDEs Burgers, Schrodinger, Kovasznay, Beltrami; 3 seeds. At equal iterations with two losses: "only our ConFIG and PCGrad consistently outperform the Adam baseline". — [arXiv 2408.11104v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11104v3); [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/94e85561a342de88b559b72c9b29f638-Abstract-Conference.html)
- ConFIG is one of the few weighting papers with an explicit equal wall-clock comparison: with a fixed time budget the momentum variant "M-ConFIG outperforms all other methods, even PCGrad and the regular ConFIG method", and plain ConFIG "still outperforms Adam despite its higher computational cost for each iteration" on Beltrami. The same paper reports that on a 10-task CelebA problem at equal time M-ConFIG (F1 0.536) loses to ConFIG (0.635), i.e. the advantage degrades as the number of loss terms grows. PINN MSE numbers are in figures/appendix that I did not retrieve. — [arXiv 2408.11104v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11104v3)
- Wang et al., "Gradient Alignment in PINNs: A Second-Order Optimization Perspective" (arXiv February 2025; described in a search summary as a NeurIPS 2025 paper, venue not verified by me): argues that quasi-second-order methods resolve conflicts between loss-term gradients and outperform Adam, i.e. that weighting problems are partly an optimizer problem. — [arXiv 2502.00604](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00604v2)
- Maddu et al., inverse-Dirichlet weighting (arXiv 2107.00940): addresses a failure mode from multi-scale dynamics with scale imbalance between loss terms. — [ar5iv 2107.00940](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.00940)
- Learned or search-based weighting found: (i) Psaros, Kawaguchi, Karniadakis meta-learn the loss function and optionally the term weights offline (see section 3); (ii) Loss-Attentional PINN (Song et al., cited in the Torres et al. survey) uses loss-attentional networks trained by gradient ascent for point-wise weights; (iii) a AAAI 2026 paper (Wu and Wu, "MOO-VARI") runs NSGA-II over the Pareto front and converts it to weights, claiming to "consistently outperform" standard PINN and other adaptive schemes on its own examples. — [arXiv 2503.18181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18181); [AAAI article 39900](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39900)
- Kendall-style uncertainty weights (one trainable log-variance per loss term) continue to be reused in PINN variants. — [arXiv 2509.25262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25262)
- A targeted search for an RL agent that sets PINN loss weights returned no such paper; the closest RL-controller work on PINNs is AutoPINN (2022), whose controller proposes architectures, not weights or schedules. — [arXiv 2212.04058](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04058)
- A 2026 preprint notes a failure mode of adaptive schemes, "loss-weight collapse", in which one loss term drives the optimizer and the rest are ignored. — [arXiv 2603.03224](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03224.pdf)

### Inferences
- RL or bandit loss weighting for PINNs appears to be genuinely unoccupied in the literature I could reach, but that is weak evidence of opportunity: the hand-designed space is dense, the rules are nearly free (ReLoBRaLo +0.6 s per 1,000 steps on Burgers), and the general multi-task literature (section 5) says tuned fixed weights are hard to beat.
- ReLoBRaLo's own tables are the best published illustration of the risk: against a grid-searched fixed-weight baseline, the adaptive method wins on some PDEs by one to two orders of magnitude and loses on others. The cost of the grid search is not charged to the baseline, but neither is hyperparameter tuning of the adaptive methods.
- The trend in 2024-2026 is to treat loss imbalance as a conditioning problem solved by curvature (Gauss-Newton, natural gradient, quasi-Newton) rather than by weights. A weighting controller attached to a second-order optimizer may have little left to fix; this should be tested before investing.
- Any learned weighting controller must be compared against: grid-searched fixed weights, LRA, NTK weights, ReLoBRaLo, SA-PINN, causal weights for time-dependent problems, and ConFIG / M-ConFIG, at equal wall-clock.

### Gaps
- Journal venues I believe but did not verify on a retrieved page: gradient pathologies (SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 2021), NTK paper (Journal of Computational Physics 2022), causal weighting (CMAME 2024).
- SA-PINN and causal-PINN numbers, and any wall-clock accounting in those papers, were not retrieved.
- I could not confirm whether any 2025-2026 paper uses a bandit over a discrete set of weighting rules (a plausible, cheap design); none surfaced.
- The Loss-Attentional PINN and MOO-VARI primary texts were not read; claims are from a survey and an abstract.

## 3. Learned optimizers and meta-learning for PINNs: do they transfer across equations?

### Takeaway
Meta-learning for PINNs is mostly about initialisations and hypernetworks within one parametrised PDE family; cross-equation transfer of a learned training rule has two data points: Bihlo's learned optimizer (advection to Burgers: early gain, then oscillation and stagnation, described by the author as proof-of-concept) and Metamizer (a neural optimizer for grid-based physics losses that claims generalisation to Poisson, wave and Burgers after training on other PDEs, but is not a PINN optimizer). None accounts for meta-training cost in the comparison, and none compares against L-BFGS or second-order baselines.

### Cited Findings

- Alex Bihlo, "Improving physics-informed neural networks with meta-learned optimization", JMLR vol. 25, 2024 (PR). Learned optimizer: MLP with two hidden layers of 32 units, 2,115 parameters, component-wise update in the form of Harrison et al. (an Adam-like term plus a black-box term), inputs are weights, gradients, second-moment accumulators and time-step features. Meta-trained with persistent evolution strategies, 20 tasks per equation (random initialisations), 50 meta-epochs, initialised close to Adam. — [JMLR v25 23-0356](https://jmlr.org/papers/v25/23-0356.html); [ar5iv 2303.07127](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.07127)
- Bihlo results vs Adam (the only baseline; no L-BFGS): linear advection "a training loss and point-wise error that is more than 10 times smaller", with loss after 500 epochs below Adam's after 3,000; KdV loss after 200 epochs below Adam's final loss at 1,000; networks are small (20 units per layer, 2 to 6 layers, 10,000 PDE points). No repeated-seed statistics; meta-training cost is not counted in the comparison (the paper claims "minimal computational overhead" without timing). — [ar5iv 2303.07127](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.07127)
- Bihlo transfer: (a) within an equation class, KdV across initial conditions (75 tasks, unseen test condition): loss after 200 epochs "comparable" to Adam after 1,000. (b) across equations, an optimizer meta-trained on linear advection applied to Burgers beat Adam for the first 400 epochs, showed "substantially higher oscillations" than the Burgers-trained optimizer, and its loss "seems to have stagnated after about 600 epochs", ending only slightly below Adam. The author calls the transfer results "proof-of-concept". — [ar5iv 2303.07127](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.07127)
- Psaros, Kawaguchi, Karniadakis, "Meta-learning PINN loss functions", Journal of Computational Physics 458, 111121, 2022 (PR). Gradient-based bi-level meta-learning of a parametrised loss (an adaptive robust loss with shape and scale, or a feed-forward network loss; term weights can also be meta-learned). Task distributions: discontinuous function approximation, advection with varying initial conditions, steady reaction-diffusion with varying source, Burgers with varying viscosity. Baselines: MSE, L1, Cauchy, Geman-McClure and an online adaptive loss. Claims "significant performance improvement" including on out-of-distribution test tasks and different PINN architectures. — [arXiv 2107.05544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05544v1); [ar5iv 2107.05544](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.05544)
- Psaros et al. limits: each experiment varies parameters within a single PDE family, so there is no cross-equation transfer evidence in the text I read; differentiating through the inner optimisation can produce exploding gradients (clipping needed); longer inner unrolls cost more; meta-training cost is moved offline, not eliminated. — [ar5iv 2107.05544](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.05544)
- Wandel, Schulz, Klein, "Metamizer", ICLR 2025 (PR). A neural optimizer with a scale-invariant architecture that iteratively minimises a physics-based loss; trained on Laplace, advection-diffusion, incompressible Navier-Stokes and cloth; the abstract states "the model also generalizes to PDEs that were not covered during training" (Poisson, wave, Burgers) and claims accuracy sometimes approaching machine precision. No numbers on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2410.19746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19746)
- Penwarden, Zhe, Narayan, Kirby, "A Metalearning Approach for PINNs: Application to Parameterized PDEs", Journal of Computational Physics 477, 111912, 2023 (PR). Model-aware metalearning of initial weights for parametrised PDEs; a search summary of the paper reports that MAML struggles in the regime where PINNs are fine-tuned with L-BFGS, while their method performs better at lower cost. — [arXiv 2110.13361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13361)
- Liu et al., "A novel meta-learning initialization method for PINNs" (NRPINN): Reptile initialisation extended to unsupervised and semi-supervised task losses. — [arXiv 2107.10991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.10991)
- Iwata et al., "Meta-learning of PINNs for Efficiently Solving Newly Given PDEs" (arXiv 2310.13270, preprint): encoder-decoder that predicts solutions without gradient-descent adaptation and can share knowledge across PDE problems "if their governing equations are formulated as polynomial functions of partial derivatives"; it notes that earlier meta-learning shares knowledge across a narrow range, e.g. Burgers with different parameters. — [arXiv 2310.13270](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13270v1)
- Hypernetwork line: Cho et al. hypernetwork meta-learning for low-rank PINNs (uses MAML and Reptile as baselines); HyPINO (arXiv September 2025) maps PDE specifications to PINN parameters, claims zero-shot generalisation and that its outputs fine-tune more efficiently than random or meta-learned initialisations; GPT-PINN (Chen and Koohy) for parametric PDEs. — [arXiv 2310.09528](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09528); [arXiv 2509.05117](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05117v1); [arXiv 2303.14878](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14878)
- Torres, Schiefer, Niepert, "Adaptive Physics-informed Neural Networks: A Survey" (arXiv March 2025, preprint) organises PINN meta-learning by what is meta-learned: initial weights (MAML, Reptile, hypernetworks), architecture, loss functions (Psaros et al.; LA-PINN), and sampling (difficulty-aware task sampling, Toloubidokhti et al.; a meta-learned KRnet sampler). In my keyword scan of the full text, reinforcement learning appears only in the context of architecture search, and learned optimizers are not a category. The survey also notes that PINNs with meta-learning still do not match optimised numerical solvers in computational efficiency. — [arXiv 2503.18181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18181)
- Evolutionary alternatives to gradient-based meta-learning for PINNs exist (Baldwin-effect PINNs; Evo-PINN survey). — [arXiv 2312.03243](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03243); [arXiv 2501.06572](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06572v4)

### Inferences
- The published answer to "do they transfer across equations?" is: weakly and with degradation. Bihlo's cross-equation result is the cleanest test and shows the classic learned-optimizer failure, good short-horizon behaviour that does not extend beyond the meta-training unroll (meta-trained at 50 epochs, tested beyond 1,000).
- Every positive result here uses Adam as the baseline. Since Adam followed by L-BFGS, and more recently quasi-Newton and natural-gradient methods, are orders of magnitude better than Adam on PINN losses (section 6), a learned first-order optimizer that is "10 times better than Adam" may still lose to an untuned L-BFGS.
- Meta-training cost is never amortised in the reported comparisons. A credible protocol must state how many target solves are needed before the meta-training cost is repaid.
- Learned initialisations and hypernetworks are the mature part of this area, but they answer a different question (many-query parametric PDEs) from a training controller.

### Gaps
- Metamizer's quantitative results on held-out PDEs, and how they compare with multigrid or conjugate-gradient baselines at equal time, were not retrieved; it also operates on discretised fields, so transfer of its conclusions to PINNs is my assumption.
- HyperPINN (de Avila Belbute-Peres et al.) and Meta-PDE (Qin et al.) primary pages were not retrieved; authorship is from memory and not verified.
- I did not find a general-purpose learned optimizer (for example VeLO-class) evaluated on PINN losses; whether such an evaluation exists is unknown.
- Penwarden et al.'s MAML finding is from a search summary, not from the paper text.

## 4. RL or learned policies for damping, trust-region radius or step size in Levenberg-Marquardt / Gauss-Newton and quasi-Newton methods (general numerical optimisation)

### Takeaway
Prior art exists but is thin: an RL controller for LM damping dates to NeurIPS 2008 (Ruvolo, Fasel, Movellan) and a 2023 bundle-adjustment paper learns the damping with SAC; both beat only the simplest multiply/divide rule (not Nielsen's rule, not a ratio-based trust-region update), and gains shrink to 5-20 % when the comparison is restricted to what LM can do. Learned step sizes for L-BFGS/BFGS exist with mixed results against a backtracking line search. I found nothing learned for damping in PINN second-order optimizers.

### Cited Findings

- Ruvolo, Fasel, Movellan, "Optimization on a Budget: A Reinforcement Learning Approach", NeurIPS 2008 (PR). Least-Squares Policy Iteration over hand-built basis functions; state = a window (size 1 or 2) of whether recent steps increased or decreased the error, plus remaining function-evaluation budget; 8 actions (keep / multiply / divide λ, combined with keeping or discarding the last step, plus two actions LM does not have: jump to a random point, or return to the best point and take an LM step). Baseline: standard LM, which divides λ by a constant on success and multiplies on failure. — [NeurIPS 2008 paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2008/file/9a96876e2f8f3dc4f3cf45f02c61c0c1-Paper.pdf)
- Ruvolo et al. numbers: on classical nonlinear least-squares test problems with a budget of 5 function evaluations, a "6% gain in performance (measured in total reduction in loss from the starting point)" over LM. On a smile-detection feature-fitting task, 2.66-fold greater loss reduction than LM with the random-restart action, but only "a 20% greater reduction in total loss" when the restart action is removed, which the authors themselves call the fairer comparison. Cross-generalisation: the controller learned on classical problems gives a 19 % improvement on the vision task; the reverse direction gives 5 %. The learned policy's weights reproduce LM's own logic (raise damping after an error increase, lower it after a decrease). — [NeurIPS 2008 paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2008/file/9a96876e2f8f3dc4f3cf45f02c61c0c1-Paper.pdf)
- Belder, Vivanti, Tal, "A Game of Bundle Adjustment - Learning Efficient Convergence" (arXiv 2308.13270, August 2023; the retrieved page names no venue). Soft Actor-Critic; state = last 5 estimation errors; action = continuous λ ≥ 0; reward = negative iteration duration plus a bonus of 10 on convergence. Trained on small synthetic scenes (10 poses, 10 points) and applied to KITTI and BAL. — [ar5iv 2308.13270](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.13270)
- Belder et al. numbers (iterations / average seconds): KITTI classic LM 75 / 340.0 vs learned 14 / 100.2; BAL classic 20 / 110.0 vs learned 4 / 62.04; Ceres 20 / 46.7 vs learned + Ceres 4 / 20.75; DeepLM (Huang et al.) 15 / 40.75 vs learned 5 / 20.3. The baseline rule is λ multiplied by 2 or 1/2 depending on whether error decreased, initial λ = 1/4, in a Python LM; Nielsen's rule or a gain-ratio trust-region update is not among the baselines. A constant-λ scheduler took 12 iterations (80.1 s) on BAL. On a small problem (5 points, 5 poses) the learned method needed 4 iterations vs 5 but about 0.5 s more total time because of agent inference. — [ar5iv 2308.13270](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.13270)
- Egidio, Hansson, Wahlberg, "Learning the Step-size Policy for the L-BFGS Algorithm" (arXiv 2010.01311): a neural step-size policy trained by backpropagation through time (not RL) on MNIST/CIFAR-10 training problems. Abstract versions disagree: v2 says it beats ADAM, RMSprop, L-BFGS with backtracking line search and L-BFGS with a constant step; an earlier version says it performs comparably to L-BFGS with backtracking line search. — [arXiv 2010.01311v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01311v2); earlier wording at [DeepAI listing](https://deepai.org/publication/learning-the-step-size-policy-for-the-limited-memory-broyden-fletcher-goldfarb-shanno-algorithm)
- Lin, Song, Xu, "A Learn-to-Optimize Approach for Coordinate-Wise Step Sizes for Quasi-Newton Methods" (arXiv 2412.00059, preprint): learned coordinate-wise step sizes for BFGS-type methods; reports up to 4x faster convergence than backtracking line search and hypergradient descent. — [arXiv 2412.00059](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00059v2)
- Transtrum and Sethna, "Improvements to the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm for nonlinear least-squares minimization": the hand-designed state of the art (delayed gratification damping updates, geodesic acceleration) that any learned damping policy should be compared against. — [arXiv 1201.5885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.5885)
- In PINNs, LM and Gauss-Newton are used with conventional adaptive damping: "The Challenges of the Nonlinear Regime for PINNs" (2024; authors not verified on a retrieved page) uses an LM variant whose damping is adjusted by the algorithm and credits it with stability over plain Gauss-Newton; a 2026 preprint (TINNs) also applies LM with adaptive damping. — [arXiv 2402.03864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03864); [arXiv 2601.20361](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20361)
- A targeted search for a learned policy that outputs a trust-region radius for general nonlinear least squares, or a learned-versus-hand-tuned radius comparison on standard test sets, returned nothing beyond the papers above. — [search hit: arXiv 2308.13270](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13270)

### Inferences
- The question "does a learned damping policy beat Marquardt or Nielsen rules?" has not been answered in the literature I reached. Both RL papers compare against the fixed-factor accept/reject rule (factor of 2, or a constant η). Neither tests Nielsen's smooth gain-ratio update, a ratio-based trust-region rule, Transtrum-Sethna's delayed gratification, or a tuned initial λ. The Belder et al. baseline is a strong candidate for a weak baseline in the McGreivy-Hakim sense: a constant λ already cut BAL iterations from 20 to 12.
- The 2008 result is the most honest sizing of the prize: once the comparison is restricted to LM's own action set, the learned controller gains 6 % to 20 % in loss reduction under a tight evaluation budget, and the learned policy rediscovers the heuristic. Large gains came from actions outside the method (restarts), which matches the user's interest in restarts as a controller action.
- Cross-domain generalisation of a damping controller is plausible because the state can be made problem-agnostic (signs or ratios of recent error changes, gain ratio, remaining budget). Ruvolo et al. show asymmetric transfer (19 % one way, 5 % the other); Belder et al. show synthetic-to-real transfer inside one problem class.
- For PINNs specifically, damping control for Gauss-Newton / LM / natural-gradient optimizers with a learned policy appears to be unoccupied. The necessary comparison set is: Marquardt factor rule, Nielsen's rule, gain-ratio trust region, Transtrum-Sethna, and a tuned constant damping, at equal Jacobian-vector products or wall-clock.

### Gaps
- Publication venue of Belder et al. not confirmed.
- I did not find, and so cannot cite, comparisons of learned damping against Nielsen's rule specifically; absence is from two searches.
- Dynamic algorithm configuration literature (RL control of step size in CMA-ES and similar) is likely relevant prior art for the evaluation methodology but did not surface in my searches; not covered.
- Whether Lin et al. (2412.00059) use RL or supervised unrolling was not confirmed.

## 5. Negative or null results and benchmark critiques

### Takeaway
There is no single definitive null result for adaptive PINN training, but the pieces add up to a strong prior against unqualified claims: independent benchmarks find task-specific gains with real overhead, paper-to-paper rankings of the same methods flip, the multi-task literature finds that specialised gradient/weighting methods do not beat tuned scalarisation, and McGreivy and Hakim document that 79 % of ML-for-PDE papers claiming superiority used weak baselines.

### Cited Findings

- McGreivy and Hakim, "Weak baselines and reporting biases lead to overoptimism in machine learning for fluid-related PDEs", Nature Machine Intelligence 6, 1256-1269, 2024 (PR). 79 % (60/76) of articles claiming to outperform a standard numerical method compared against a weak baseline; outcome-reporting and publication bias are widespread; proposed rules: compare at equal accuracy or equal runtime, and against an efficient numerical method. — [arXiv 2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1); [EurekAlert press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1058911)
- PINNacle, NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks (PR): 22 PDE cases, 20,000 epochs at learning rate 1e-3, three repeats; compares vanilla PINN, PINN-w, L-BFGS, PINN-LRA, PINN-NTK, RAR, MultiAdam, gPINN, hp-VPINN, LAAF, GAAF, FBPINN. Vanilla PINN "only solves 10 out of 22 tasks" at a 10 % L2RE threshold; "Methods involving loss reweighting and resampling have shown improved performance in some cases", but "these methods still struggle with Wave equations, Navier-Stokes equations". Comparisons are at equal epochs, not equal time. — [ar5iv 2306.08827](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.08827); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/8c63299fb2820ef41cb05e2ff11836f5-Abstract.html)
- PINNacle runtime (seconds, 20,000 epochs, mean of three runs): Burgers 1d-C vanilla 284, LRA 764, NTK 670, MultiAdam 506; Poisson 2d-C vanilla 339, LRA 901, NTK 809; Heat 2d-VC vanilla 1160, LRA 3520, NTK 1690; Wave 1d-C vanilla 350, LRA 1120, NTK 840; KS vanilla 1400, LRA 3170, NTK 3590. "All other methods introduced varying degrees of additional computational overhead compared to vanilla PINN." PINN-NTK diverged (NaN) at learning rate 1e-2 and PINN-LRA diverged on NS2d-C; the best reweighting momentum depends on the problem. — [ar5iv 2306.08827](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.08827)
- Florido et al. (ICCS 2024): at a fixed number of residual evaluations, several adaptive-sampling guiding metrics help, but no single approach is likely to be optimal. — [arXiv 2404.12282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12282)
- Internal inconsistency between method papers (see section 1): RAD "has always performed the best among the 10 sampling methods" in Wu et al., but in Daw et al. RAD reaches 64.1 ± 1.98 % on convection β=50 where uniform resampling reaches 56.9 ± 9.08 % and R3 1.98 ± 0.72 %, and in RL-PINNs RAD is worse than uniform on the dual-peak problem (1.0889 vs 0.8624). — [ar5iv 2207.10289](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10289); [ar5iv 2207.02338](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.02338); [arXiv 2504.12949](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12949v1)
- Uniform resampling as a strong free baseline: Random-R is "usually the best uniform method" in Wu et al. and matches the best adaptive samplers on Allen-Cahn in Daw et al. (0.77 ± 0.06 vs R3 0.83 ± 0.15). — [ar5iv 2207.10289](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10289); [ar5iv 2207.02338](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.02338)
- ReLoBRaLo's tables contain cases where a grid-searched fixed-weight baseline beats adaptive methods (inverse Burgers: fixed 1.9e-10 vs ReLoBRaLo 2.2e-10; Burgers forward: fixed 1.2e-3 vs GradNorm 2.0e-3) despite the abstract's "consistently outperform" claim. — [ar5iv 2110.09813](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.09813)
- General multi-task learning, directly analogous to PINN loss balancing: Kurin, De Palma, Kostrikov, Whiteson, Kumar, "In Defense of the Unitary Scalarization for Deep Multi-Task Learning", NeurIPS 2022 (PR): minimising the plain sum of task losses with standard regularisation matches or beats specialised multi-task optimizers. Xin, Ghorbani, Garg, Firat, Gilmer, "Do Current Multi-Task Optimization Methods in Deep Learning Even Help?" (arXiv 2209.11379): "MTO methods do not yield any performance improvements beyond what is achievable via traditional optimization approaches." — [arXiv 2201.04122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04122); [arXiv 2209.11379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11379)
- The optimizer matters more than the add-ons: Rathore, Lei, Frangella, Lu, Udell, "Challenges in Training PINNs: A Loss Landscape Perspective", ICML 2024 oral (PR). Best L2RE over widths, learning rates and 5 seeds at 41,000 iterations: convection Adam 5.96e-2, L-BFGS 8.26e-3, Adam+L-BFGS 4.19e-3; wave Adam 3.49e-1, L-BFGS 3.35e-1, Adam+L-BFGS 5.52e-2. A further NysNewton-CG phase reduces loss by more than 10x in every case (wave L2RE 5.52e-2 to 1.27e-2) at a per-iteration cost of about 5.4x (convection), 20x (reaction) and 322x (wave) relative to L-BFGS. The loss is "still under-optimized" after Adam+L-BFGS. — [ar5iv 2402.01868](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.01868); [PMLR v235 rathore24a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/rathore24a.html)
- Urban, Stefanou, Pons, "Unveiling the optimization process of PINNs: How accurate and competitive can PINNs be?", Journal of Computational Physics 523, 113656, 2025 (PR): choosing an improved (quasi-Newton) optimizer has a larger effect on convergence than loss modifications; together they reduce the loss by several orders of magnitude, with 2-3 layer, 20-30 neuron networks. — [arXiv 2405.04230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04230)
- Wang, Sankaran, Wang, Perdikaris, "An Expert's Guide to Training PINNs" (arXiv 2308.08468, preprint): a hand-assembled training pipeline with ablations and a JAX library; in practice this is the tuned-human baseline a learned controller must beat. — [arXiv 2308.08468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08468)
- Turbulence-modelling PINN study: the gap between optimised fixed weights and adaptive weighting was reported as minimal, with the adaptive method's benefit being the avoided grid search. — [arXiv 2306.01065](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.01065) (finding as summarised by search; not verified in full text)

### Inferences
- The defensible reading is not "adaptive methods do not work" but "adaptive methods are problem-specific, cost 1.5-3x per epoch when they need per-term gradients or NTK quantities, and are usually evaluated at equal epochs against under-tuned baselines". A learned controller inherits all of these evaluation problems plus meta-training cost.
- The largest documented gains in PINN training in 2024-2026 come from switching optimizer class (Adam to quasi-Newton / Newton-CG / natural gradient), not from sampling or weighting. This shifts where a controller could matter: scheduling the switch (Rathore et al. found the Adam to L-BFGS switch point to be a tuned hyperparameter with three candidate values), deciding when to pay for 5-322x more expensive Newton-CG iterations, damping, and restarts when the line search stalls (L-BFGS "often stops early" because the strong Wolfe search fails).
- McGreivy-Hakim's two rules translate directly to this setting: (1) report error at equal wall-clock or equal residual and Jacobian evaluations, including controller inference and amortised meta-training; (2) the baseline must be an efficient one, meaning tuned fixed weights, uniform resampling and Adam followed by L-BFGS at minimum.

### Gaps
- I did not find a peer-reviewed PINN-specific paper whose headline claim is a null result for adaptive weighting or sampling against tuned baselines. The search for one returned mixed, mostly positive single-group results. Such a study may be absent because of the reporting bias that McGreivy and Hakim describe, or I may have missed it.
- PINNacle's main accuracy table was garbled in extraction; I cannot give per-method win counts, and RAR's runtime is not in its runtime table.
- The McGreivy-Hakim numbers are from the arXiv version and press coverage, not the published article page.
- The turbulence-model finding is from a search summary and should be checked before being cited.

## 6. What remains open

### Takeaway
Three things appear open on the evidence I could gather: a training controller meta-trained on a set of equations and evaluated on held-out equations with multi-seed, equal-compute reporting; any learned control of damping, switching or restarts for second-order PINN optimizers; and an agreed evaluation protocol. The second is the most defensible bet because that is where the documented gains and the untuned discrete decisions are.

### Cited Findings

- Cross-equation generalisation of a learned training rule has been tested only as proof-of-concept: one source equation to one target (Bihlo, JMLR 2024: early gain then stagnation after about 600 epochs), and for a grid-based neural optimizer rather than PINNs (Metamizer, ICLR 2025). — [ar5iv 2303.07127](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.07127); [arXiv 2410.19746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19746)
- The one RL sampler with reported numbers trains its agent per run and lists transfer across equations as future work. — [arXiv 2504.12949](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12949v1)
- Meta-learning for PINNs has been organised around initial weights, architecture, loss functions and task/point sampling; learned control of optimizer hyperparameters is not a category in the 2025 survey, and the survey concedes that meta-learned PINNs still trail optimised numerical solvers in computational efficiency. — [arXiv 2503.18181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18181)
- Second-order and curvature-aware PINN optimizers are an active 2024-2026 front with hand-set control: NysNewton-CG (Rathore et al., ICML 2024); LM with algorithmic damping (arXiv 2402.03864, 2024); Dual Natural Gradient Descent scaling Gauss-Newton solves to networks with up to 12.8 million parameters on one GPU (Jnini and Vella, 2025 preprint); "Curvature-Aware Optimization for High-Accuracy PINNs" (Jnini, Kiyani, Shukla, Urban, Ahmadi Daryakenari, Muller, Zeinhofer, Karniadakis, arXiv April 2026, preprint) with natural gradient, self-scaling BFGS and Broyden on Helmholtz, Stokes, inviscid Burgers, Euler and stiff ODEs, compared against high-order numerical methods. — [ar5iv 2402.01868](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.01868); [arXiv 2402.03864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03864); [arXiv 2505.21404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21404v1); [arXiv 2604.05230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05230)
- Hand-tuned discrete decisions in those pipelines that are documented as mattering: the Adam to L-BFGS switch point (1k, 11k or 31k of 41k iterations, selected per problem), the recommendation to run Adam+L-BFGS "as far as possible" before paying for NNCG, and L-BFGS terminating early when the strong Wolfe line search fails with gradient norms still around 1e-2 to 1e-3. — [ar5iv 2402.01868](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.01868)
- Hyperparameter control for PINNs today is offline: Bayesian optimisation (BO-SA-PINNs), two-stage evolutionary HPO over schedules and learning-rate ranges (2026 preprint), an RNN controller for architecture (AutoPINN, 2022), and a loss-based learning-rate scheduler. A search for an online bandit or RL learning-rate controller for PINNs returned none. — [arXiv 2606.20442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20442); [arXiv 2212.04058](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04058); [arXiv 2504.09804](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09804v1)
- Evaluation practice in the cited method papers: equal residual points (Wu et al.), equal iterations (Daw et al., PINNacle, Rathore et al.), equal steps with overhead reported separately (ReLoBRaLo), and equal wall-clock in only one case (ConFIG); seeds range from 1 (RL-PINNs, Bihlo) to 3-5 (ConFIG, PINNacle, Daw, Rathore) to 10 or more (Wu et al.). — [ar5iv 2207.10289](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10289); [ar5iv 2207.02338](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.02338); [ar5iv 2306.08827](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.08827); [ar5iv 2110.09813](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.09813); [arXiv 2408.11104v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11104v3)
- The fair-comparison standard proposed for ML-for-PDE work is equal accuracy or equal runtime against an efficient baseline. — [arXiv 2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07218v1)

### Inferences
- Is a learned training controller a credible research bet? On this evidence it is credible only in a narrow form. Against it: hand-designed rules are nearly free and dense; published "learned" results use Adam-only or untuned baselines; the best analogue outside PINNs (LM damping by RL) yields 6-20 % when compared fairly; multi-task learning has a documented history of adaptive methods collapsing against tuned scalarisation. For it: the specific combination "controller trained across equations, tested on unseen ones" has essentially no published attempt for PINNs; controllers for second-order PINN optimizers (damping, phase switching, restarts, when to refresh curvature) are unoccupied while that optimizer class is where the order-of-magnitude gains are; and problem-agnostic state features (gain ratio, sign history of loss changes, remaining budget) have shown some cross-domain transfer.
- A null result here would itself be publishable and useful, given the reporting-bias critique, provided the protocol is strong. A bandit over a small set of known rules (Marquardt, Nielsen, trust-region ratio; uniform resampling, RAD, R3; fixed, LRA, ReLoBRaLo) is the lowest-risk design because it cannot do much worse than the best arm and has negligible inference cost.
- Minimum comparison set implied by the literature. Sampling: fixed Hammersley/Sobol, uniform resampling (Random-R), RAD, RAR-D, R3. Weighting: grid-searched fixed weights, LRA, NTK, ReLoBRaLo, SA-PINN, causal weights (time-dependent), ConFIG / M-ConFIG. Optimizer: Adam with a tuned schedule, Adam followed by L-BFGS with a tuned switch, self-scaling BFGS / Broyden, LM or Gauss-Newton with Nielsen and gain-ratio damping, NysNewton-CG or natural gradient where affordable. Reference: a classical solver at matched accuracy for context.
- Minimum protocol implied by the critiques: error versus wall-clock and versus residual / Jacobian-vector-product evaluations (not epochs); controller inference charged; meta-training cost reported with a break-even number of target solves; at least 5 seeds with dispersion; baselines tuned with the same budget as the controller; a held-out set of equations fixed before meta-training (different operator class, not only different coefficients); results reported for every equation including losses.

### Gaps
- I cannot prove a negative: "no published controller trained across equations and tested on unseen ones" and "no learned controller for second-order PINN optimizers" rest on roughly 20 searches and the 2025 survey's taxonomy. Work in late 2026, workshop papers and non-arXiv venues may exist.
- The full text of the April 2026 curvature-aware optimisation paper (2604.05230) was not read, so I do not know whether it already addresses damping schedules or phase switching in a way that pre-empts a controller; a search summary says natural gradient often converges fastest on elliptic problems but is very compute-intensive.
- No source gives a community-agreed PINN training benchmark protocol with equal-compute rules; PINNacle fixes epochs. Whether a successor benchmark has adopted wall-clock budgets is unknown.
- Kiyani et al. (2025) on optimizer choice for PINNs and KANs, and Muller and Zeinhofer's energy natural gradient (ICML 2023), are relevant to the second-order baseline set but were not retrieved and are not cited above.
